# Rules: Roofing (generic compliance and domain rules)

These are working rules, not legal, insurance, engineering or tax advice. Local law, the adopted code edition, the policy, the contract and the manufacturer instructions control. Items tagged **VERIFY locally** came from general knowledge or sources that conflict; confirm with {{LOCAL_CODE_OFFICE}}, {{COUNSEL}} or the manufacturer before relying on them. Fill `{{STATE}}` specifics in `{{STATE_RULES}}`.

## Tier 1 (immutable)
1. **Never waive, rebate, absorb or "cover" an insurance deductible**, directly or through discounts, gifts, inflated invoices or promised credits. Several states make this illegal (VERIFY {{STATE}}). No canvass script, ad, text or proposal may suggest it. Gate: `../skills/deductible-and-inducement-gate/SKILL.md`.
2. **No unlicensed adjusting.** The office documents damage and compares scopes. It does not negotiate the claim, interpret coverage, or advise on legal rights unless the person holds the required license. Some states restrict contractors from negotiating claims at all (VERIFY {{STATE}}).
3. **Fall protection is not optional.** Work on a residential roof at 4 ft or more above the lower level needs guardrails, safety nets or personal fall arrest unless infeasible; then a written site-specific plan and training apply (VERIFY against current OSHA text and any state plan).
4. **Written contract before work, signed by both parties.** Contents listed below.
5. **Human sign-off before anything leaves the office:** contracts, estimates, claim documents, supplement letters, lien waivers, warranty certificates, and outbound marketing.
6. **Verify, do not generate.** Quantities, money and dates come from `../harnesses/` or a source document, never from model arithmetic.
7. **Photos and records are evidence.** Do not alter, stage or reuse photos from another property. Edits are limited to labeling and cropping, with originals preserved.

## Inspection access and safety
- OSHA guidance has addressed inspection or assessment work separately from construction work. Treat any fall-protection relief for brief inspection or assessment as narrow: **VERIFY locally** with the current OSHA FAQ text and your state plan; when in doubt, use fall protection or a drone/ground assessment.
- Do not walk a roof that is wet, icy, damaged, or steeper than the crew can safely work. Use ground, ladder, or drone documentation instead.
- Keep distance from power lines; treat all lines as energized.

Required text is state-specific (VERIFY {{STATE}}).

## Cancellation and sales rules
- Door-to-door and off-premises sales commonly carry a statutory cancellation right (often at least three business days). VERIFY locally.
- Some states give an extra cancellation right if the insurance claim is denied (VERIFY {{STATE}}).
- Local solicitation permits, registration, do-not-solicit signs and permitted hours apply (VERIFY {{CITY_OR_COUNTY}}).
- Deposits: state caps or trust requirements may apply (VERIFY {{STATE}}). In the books, deposits are liabilities until earned.

## Insurance claim rules
- The homeowner files and owns the claim. The contractor documents and supports.
- Payment stages: initial ACV payment; recoverable depreciation after completion; supplements (one to three is common); overhead and profit where allowed.
- Deadlines for completing work, claiming recoverable depreciation, and any proof of loss vary by policy and state. Record them in the claim file and **VERIFY locally** with the policy documents.
- Mortgagee endorsement can delay checks. Plan cash flow accordingly.
- Assignment of benefits and direction-to-pay documents are restricted in some states (VERIFY {{STATE}}). Do not use them without counsel review.
- Supplements cite evidence (photos, measurements, code, manufacturer requirement). Do not pad scope.
- Matching and appraisal disputes belong to the policyholder and qualified representatives.

## Code and installation (general; local code and manufacturer instructions control)
- Re-roof permit and inspections as required by {{LOCAL_CODE_OFFICE}}.
- Maximum two layers of asphalt shingle in the model residential code; tear-off is required when deck, layer count, or manufacturer rules demand it (VERIFY local amendment).
- Deck inspected and repaired or re-nailed before dry-in; damaged decking replaced.
- Ice barrier from the eave edge to at least 24 in inside the exterior wall line where the code requires it (cold-climate jurisdictions; VERIFY edition and map).
- Underlayment laps: about 2 in side laps and 4 in end laps, with end laps offset about 6 ft (VERIFY the edition and the product data sheet).
- Asphalt shingles are not installed below 2:12. Slopes from 2:12 up to 4:12 need double underlayment or a code-approved special underlayment (VERIFY edition).
- Flashing: step flashing at walls, kickouts, valley treatment per manufacturer, penetration flashing, drip edge at eaves and rakes.
- Ventilation: balanced intake and exhaust; the net free area ratio is commonly 1/150 or 1/300 of attic floor area depending on conditions (VERIFY edition and whether a vapor retarder or balanced vents allow 1/300).
- Wind and fire classification of the product must match the code and local wind speed requirement (VERIFY).
- Dry-in and final inspections are photo-documented with date stamps.
- Manufacturer installation instructions control nailing pattern, nail count and placement. The office does not invent specs; product-specific install specs are an open gap (see `research-notes.md`).

## Safety program (OSHA-based; VERIFY current text)
- Written safety program, hazard communication program, and incident reporting.
- Fall protection plan for each roof, training records, daily toolbox talks.
- Harness fit inspected each day; anchors rated and installed to instructions; rope grabs, lifelines and self-retracting lifelines inspected before use.
- Ladders: extend about 3 ft above the landing, secured, set at the proper angle, inspected.
- Heat illness prevention: water, shade, rest, acclimatization.
- Electrical and power-line clearance; nail gun safety; hot work and torch safety for membrane work.
- Reporting thresholds for injuries and fatalities follow current OSHA rules (VERIFY).
- Blog and vendor figures on fall statistics and fines conflict; cite OSHA text only.

## Subcontractor rules
- Before any work: license (where required), certificate of insurance with {{COI_MINIMUMS}}, workers compensation status, references and sample photos, signed sub agreement, safety orientation.
- Payment by milestone with holdback: dry-in inspection passed, photo-documented final, punch closed. Piece rate or day rate is set in the agreement.
- Employee versus contractor classification is a legal question (VERIFY with {{COUNSEL}}).

## Closeout, liens and money
- Final invoice promptly after final acceptance.
- Lien waivers: conditional or partial with progress payments; final with final payment. Statutory forms are required in some states (VERIFY {{STATE}}).
- Preliminary or notice-of-commencement requirements may apply (VERIFY).
- Deposits are liabilities until earned. Do not spend customer deposits on other jobs without owner approval.
- Recoverable-depreciation release requests include completion documents, photos, final invoice and any carrier forms.

## Warranty
- Three layers: installer workmanship warranty; manufacturer material warranty; extended or system warranty (certified applicator, registration required).
- Registration commonly must be filed within about 30 days of completion (VERIFY the manufacturer program).
- Common exclusions: installation by others, storm or impact damage, lack of maintenance, foot traffic, unapproved modifications.
- Commercial: no-dollar-limit versus labor-and-material prorated versus material-only. Terms differ; read the form.
- Keep inspection and repair records to preserve warranty validity.

## Marketing and outreach
- TCPA: texts and auto-dialed or prerecorded calls need appropriate prior consent. Missed-call text-back replies only to the caller's own inquiry and carries opt-out language (VERIFY counsel guidance).
- Do-not-call: scrub national, state and internal lists; keep evidence.
- CAN-SPAM: truthful headers and subject, physical mailing address, working opt-out.
- Referral programs: no prohibited inducements, especially tied to insurance claims or deductibles (VERIFY {{STATE}}).
- Reviews: ask all customers; do not gate, buy or script content; disclose any incentive where required.
- Before/after photos and yard signs only with written permission.
- Financing offers: disclose terms and lender identity as required; the office does not quote rates.
- Storm response messaging is local, licensed, insured and honest about timelines.

## Drones and surveys
- Commercial drone flights need a certified pilot and airspace compliance (VERIFY current aviation rules).
- A moisture survey result is a map of suspected wet areas. It is verified by probes or cores before scope is final.

## AI and automation rules
- AI output is a draft. A person verifies photo assessments, labels, measurements and supplement gaps.
- Automated first responses disclose that they are automated if required (VERIFY) and always offer a human.
- Vendor claims about response-time statistics are unverified; do not quote them.
- Never ask an AI assistant to produce a legal opinion, coverage opinion, or engineering sign-off.
