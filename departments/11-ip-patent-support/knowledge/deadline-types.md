# Deadline Types (orientation only)

These are general descriptions for recognizing what a deadline in a counsel email probably is. They are not a calculator. Dates, jurisdictions and exceptions vary. Always take the date from counsel or the filing record, and confirm with counsel (VERIFY).

| Kind | General description | Who supplies the date |
|---|---|---|
| Provisional conversion | A US provisional application lapses unless a nonprovisional claiming it is filed about 12 months after the provisional filing date. | counsel |
| Paris priority | Foreign filings that claim an earlier application generally must follow within about 12 months (patents) or 6 months (marks). | counsel |
| PCT national phase | Entry into national or regional phases generally falls around 30 months from the earliest priority date; some offices differ. | counsel |
| Office action response | An examiner's action sets a response period; extensions may cost fees. | counsel, from the action |
| US public-disclosure grace period | The US allows a limited period after the inventor's own public disclosure to file; many other countries do not. Treat any planned disclosure as a counsel question first. | counsel |
| Maintenance fees | Granted patents need periodic fees to stay in force. | counsel or docketing service |
| Trademark use and renewal filings | Registered marks need declarations of continued use and renewals at set intervals. | counsel |
| Domain, hosting and certificate renewals | Not legal deadlines, but loss can harm the brand. | office record |
